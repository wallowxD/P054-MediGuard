### Pregnancy and lactation:
*Pregnancy:*
Thiazide, thiazide diuretics and loop diuretics cross the placental barrier appear in cord blood and cause alterations of fluid and electrolyte balance in the fetus. Many cases of neonatal thrombocytopenia have been reported associated with thiazide and its derivatives. This risk also appears after the use of loop diuretics such as furosemide and bumetanide.
Furosemide should only be used during pregnancy when the benefits to the mother outweigh the risk to the fetus.
Women of childbearing potential or using birth control methods should consult their doctor carefully before taking this medicine.

*Lactation:*
The use of furosemide during lactation may lead to the risk of inhibiting breast milk production. Furosemide is not recommended during lactation.

### Effects on ability to drive and use machines:
Information on effects of furosemide on ability to drive and use machines has not been reported, however, caution should be exercised during the treatment with furosemide because side effects may occur, including headache, hypotension and muscle cramp. Furosemide may reduce alertness and affects the ability to drive and operate machinery.

### Interactions, incompatibilities of medicine:
**Interaction with other medicinal product and other forms of interaction:**
- *Antihypertensives:* Enhance hypotensive effect. If coadministration of furosemide and antihypertensive is required, dosage should be adjusted. Particularly, concurrent use with angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors can result in severe hypotension.
- *Anti-arrhythmics (including amiodarone, disopyramide, flecainide and sotalol):* Risk of cardiac toxicity (because of furosemide-induced hypokalemia). The effects of lidocaine, tocainide or mexiletine may be antagonised by furosemide.
- *Drugs that prolong Q-T interval:* Increase risk of cardiac toxicity with furosemide-induced hypokalemia and/or hypomagnesemia.
- *Cardiac glycosides:* Furosemide-induced hypokalemia increase the risk of cardiac toxicity. Blood potassium levels and ECG should be monitored.
- *Vasodilators:* Enhance hypotensive effect with moxisylyte (thymoxamine) or hydralazine.
- *Renin inhibitors:* Aliskiren reduces plasma furosemide concentrations.
- *Nitrates:* Enhance hypotensive effect.
- *Other diuretics:* Increase effect of furosemide, increase risk of hypokalemia with thiazide. Concurrent use of potassium sparing diuretics and furosemide may reduce potassium depletion (positive).
- *Antibiotics:* Cephalosporin increases nephrotoxicity; aminoglycosides increase toxicity to ears and kidneys; polymyxins and vancomycin increase toxicity to ears. Furosemide can reduce serum vancomycin concentration after cardiac surgery.
- *Antifungals:* Increase risk of hypokalemia with amphotericin.
- *Lithium:* Increases blood lithium concentration, resulting in toxicity. It is not recommended if lithium concentration cannot be closely monitored.
- *Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs:* Increase risk of nephrotoxicity, reduce effect of diuretics. Indomethacin and ketorolac may antagonize effects of furosemide. In patients with dehydration or blood volume depletion, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs may cause acute renal failure.
- *Corticosteroids:* Increase risk of hypokalemia, diuretic effect antagonised by corticosteroids.
- *Salicylates:* Effects may be potentiated by furosemide.
- *Antidiabetics:* Furosemide reduces hypoglycemic effect of antidiabetic drugs, which should be monitored and the dosage should be adjusted.
- *Nondepolarizing muscle relaxants:* Increase muscle relaxant effect.

Neurological disorders: Paranaesthesia, confusion, headache.
Gastrointestinal disorders: Acute pancreatitis (in long-term treatment with diuretics, including furosemide).
Hepatobiliary disorders: Intrahepatic cholestasis (jaundice), abnormal liver function.
Renal and urinary disorders: Acute renal impairment.
General disorders: Fatigue, fever, severe anaphylactic or anaphylactoid reactions.
Tests: Increase transaminase.

*Very rare:*
Blood and lymphatic system disorders: Hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia.
Metabolism and nutrition disorders: Muscle cramps.
Renal and urinary disorders: Interstitial nephritis.

**Guidance on handling ADRs:**
ECG should be monitored regularly. Potassium supplements or concurrent use of potassium-sparing diuretics in patients at high risk of hypokalemia should be indicated. Whenever adverse reactions are moderate or severe, reduce the dosage or withdraw therapy of furosemide.

### Overdose and management:
*Overdose:*
Water and electrolyte imbalance, including headache, muscle weakness, cramps, thirst, hypotension, anorexia, tachycardia.

*Management:*
Replenish the lost electrolytes and water.
Monitor closely for timely remedial measures.

### Pharmacodynamics:
Pharmacotherapeutic group: Loop diuretics.
ATC code: C03CA01.
Furosemide is a sulfonamide diuretic with a potent and rapid action, which is dose dependent. Furosemide acts at the ascending limb of Henle's loop, so it is grouped into loop diuretics. Furosemide mainly acts by inhibiting Na⁺, K⁺, 2Cl⁻ cotransport system in the thick ascending limb of Henle's loop, which increases the excretion of these electrolytes associated with increased excretion of water. There is also an increased excretion of Ca⁺⁺ and Mg⁺⁺. Furosemide has potent diuretic effect resulting in mild hypotension. In patients with pulmonary edema, furosemide increases intravascular volume; therefore, it decreases preload on the ventricle before diuretic effects are apparent.

### Pharmacokinetics:
*Absorption:* Furosemide is well-absorbed orally, the diuretic effect of furosemide is apparent within 30 minutes and the peak concentration is achieved 1 - 2 hours after oral administration, the duration of action is 4 - 6 hours. The antihypertensive effect persisted for a longer time than diuretic effect. In patient with severe edema, furosemide bioavailability is reduced, which may be directly affected by reduced gastrointestinal absorption.
Foods may prolong and reduce the furosemide absorption.
*Distribution:* Up to 99% of furosemide is bounded to plasma albumin. The unbound fraction is found higher in patients with heart disease, renal impairment and cirrhosis. Furosemide crosses the placental barrier and is distributed into breast milk.
*Metabolism, elimination:* Furosemide is mainly excreted in the urine, mostly in the nonmetabolised form. Half-life elimination is from 30 - 120 minutes in normal people, prolonged in infants and patients with hepatic and renal impairment.
